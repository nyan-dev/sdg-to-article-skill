# Reusable SDG Citations

Use these whenever a manuscript makes an explicit UN Sustainable Development
Goal claim. They are stable, institutional, and safe to carry across any paper
or thesis — add to the reference list once per manuscript.

---

## 1. Primary — the current annual UN progress report

The official UN report on progress toward the SDGs. **Current edition: 2026.**
This is the default citation for "the SDGs are X" statements.

**In-text:** `(UN DESA, 2026)` · `(United Nations, 2026)`

**Reference list entry (numbered / APA style):**

> United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs. (2026). *The
> Sustainable Development Goals Report 2026*. United Nations, New York, NY.
> https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/

**Verified metadata**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Publisher | United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (UN DESA), Statistics Division |
| Published | 7 July 2026 |
| Extent | 48 pp. |
| ISBN | 9789211547351 |
| Series ISSN (online) | 25183958 |
| Series DOI | 10.18356/d3229fb0-en |
| Landing page | https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/ |
| Extended report | https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/extended-report/ |

**Official citation string published by DESA:**

> UN DESA. 2026. The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2026 – July 2026. New York,
> USA: UN DESA. © UN DESA. https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/.

### Rotating to a new edition
This report is annual (usually early July). When the next edition appears,
change only the year and edition number — everything else holds. Verify the ISBN
and page count against https://desapublications.un.org/publications before
committing, and update this file.

## 2. Foundational — the instrument that created the Goals

Cite this when the sentence is about the Goals *themselves* (their origin,
scope, the 2030 deadline) rather than about progress toward them. **This citation
does not go stale** and is a good second reference when a reviewer wants
authority for an SDG claim.

**In-text:** `(United Nations General Assembly, 2015)`

**Reference list entry:**

> United Nations General Assembly. (2015). *Transforming our world: the 2030
> Agenda for Sustainable Development* (Resolution A/RES/70/1). United Nations,
> New York, NY. https://undocs.org/A/RES/70/1

Adopted 25 September 2015. This resolution is the legal source of all 17 Goals.

---

## 3. Indicator framework (only if citing specific indicators)

> United Nations General Assembly. (2016). *Global indicator framework for the
> Sustainable Development Goals and the Targets* (Resolution A/RES/71/313).
> United Nations, New York, NY. https://undocs.org/A/RES/71/313

Usually unnecessary. Most manuscripts should cite 1 and/or 2 only.

---

## 4. Goal-by-goal target text

If a sentence names a sub-target (2.4, 12.3, 14.2), the wording of that target
is reproduced in `resources/sdg_targets.md`, sourced from
https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goalN. Reference 1 above covers progress reporting on
targets; cite it and no separate per-target reference is needed.

---

## Recommendation

For almost every paper, **one reference is enough**: the 2026 report (reference 1).
Add reference 2 when the Introduction frames the work against the 2030 Agenda
itself — which is common for sustainability-motivated manuscripts, so expect to
carry both.

### Example sentence, fully referenced

> Reducing feed input costs through least-cost formulation directly supports
> {food security} and {agricultural productivity} under Sustainable Development
> Goal 2, while encouraging {resource efficiency} and {sustainable food
> consumption} in line with Goal 12 (United Nations, 2015; UN DESA, 2026).

Keywords used: `{food security}` and `{agricultural productivity}` (SDG 2),
`{resource efficiency}` and `{sustainable food consumption}` (SDG 12) — all four
verified present in the INTI keyword list.
